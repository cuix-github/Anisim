# Open questions

Kept current; the answered ones stay at the bottom with their answer and date, because the answers are findings.

## Open

- **A CFL-bound substep.** Every long run steps at a fixed dt. The 512 run's air reached 900 to 1450 cells per second and crossed 76 to 123 cells in one substep, past anything the push-out reaches, which is how a handful of air particles ended up inside a boulder for one frame; the 2D long runs' splashes made particles at 120 to 460 cells per second in every arm. Fine for an A/B between solvers, not for a quality claim.
- **Animated mesh colliders.** The node moves one kind of solid since 2026-10-16: the piston wavemaker, a plate in rigid translation computed inside the step from the substep index (`gates/validate_piston_node.py`, locked against the reference's plate). A collider mesh an artist animates is not that: it needs the collider's level set per frame from the graph, a velocity from two of them, and the substep's position in between. Not needed for the wavemaker, and since 2026-10-17 not needed for a directional sea either: the wave zone (`zone_*` ports, `waves`) writes any list of oblique trains without a paddle. Open until a shot asks for a moving object in the water.
- **The wave basin's side walls and its first run.** The oblique trains reflect off the closed side walls and cross the incident trains, as a basin without absorbing beaches would; a basin fits absorbers along the sides. A zone along each side wall with a target of zero velocity (the same blend) would be the absorber, and is unbuilt. Also open: the basin's first 30 s run (`wavebasin4`, the night of 2026-10-17) has not been looked at; the trains' heights and headings are a first choice (3.2 m at 8 s at 15 degrees, 2.4 m at 6.5 s at 20 degrees the other way), to set against the look; and the tracking camera follows the highest crest, which may hand over between the two trains' crests where they are alike.
- **The step node does too much in one place.** `step_3d` carries the projection, the solids, the piston, the wave zone, escape and droplets, spray emission, ST and adaptivity behind about a hundred ports (`maya/open_sim_graph_in_maya.bat` shows it in Maya). Splitting it into nodes with one job each, chained in the loop graph, would make the graph readable and each part testable on its own; the cost is the arrays passed between them each substep. Not started (2026-10-18).
- **The wave zone's wall column.** The particles the zone carries drift forward (the Stokes drift of a prescribed progressive wave, with no return flow in a sealed box) and the wall's own column breathes by the inlet's mean flux while the wall's clamp holds what is pushed into it, so the first cells at the wall empty and water piles a few cells in (the basin's set-aside chunk 1: 10 particles where 245,000 belong, 138 percent four to eight cells in). Subtracting the Stokes drift or returning the flux through the air above the inlet both cut the radiated height by a third (the history of 2026-10-17), so neither is in. The escape test now leaves the zone's liquid alone, which keeps the void from spawning droplets, and the zone is never rendered. The fix that would remove it is a kinematic reservoir: the liquid particles within the trains' excursion of the wall placed each step at their rest lattice plus linear theory's displacement, their velocity the trains', never advected or clamped, which needs a rest position carried per particle through the loop.
- **Spray at the ceiling under the production settings.** The piston gate's hard stroke (4 m at 4 s in 20 m of water, waves of steepness 0.3 breaking at the paddle) throws escaped droplets 40 m up to the ceiling of its tank, with the air at up to 184 m/s; the plain 2D reference under a similar stroke keeps its water under 9 m/s. Escaped droplets take the air's velocity through their drag, so this is the fast-air question above seen from the spray's side, and it bears on how tall the spray of a breaker draws. Since 2026-10-16 the drag follows each droplet's own size (`droplet_model`), so the measurement is to be repeated with the model on: a millimetre drop relaxes to the air in seconds, a 10 um one in a millisecond.
- **Extra spray emission.** PF-FLIP (Section 7) emits additional spray particles near the interface where the velocity gradient is steep (Ihmsen et al. 2012), beyond the particles that escape; since 2026-10-17 ours does too (`emit_model` in `step_3d`, `Sim(emit=...)` in the reference, gated by `gates/validate_spray_node.py`), as massless droplets that the flow never feels. The paper names the method and not its thresholds, so ours are documented choices measured against the breaker's own interface gradients (10 per second selects the lip; the rate 0.05 a candidate at 30 per second emitting with 2.5 percent a step), and the water the spray draws is not taken from the liquid, which the paper does not say either way. Open: the breaker has not run with it; the night's arms (`--integrator`, `--spray`) say what it adds to the curtain, and the rate is the knob to set against the look once it has.
- **Feature-preserving surface smoothing.** ST-FLIP's Appendix B (a union of spheres of radius 0.5 dx on a grid at twice the simulation's, then k_psi = 30 iterations of mean-curvature flow under the mask g = 1 - smoothstep(2, 5; psi0 / (G ★ psi0)) with a Gaussian of 2 dx) is in `maya/particle_surface` since the night of 2026-10-16, in stock nodes (`mcf_iterations`, off by default; the gate measures the mask, its blur and the flow on a lone ball, a one-sphere sheet and a block). What stays open: the flow rounds bulk edges, 0.34 cells off the gate's block in 30 iterations, and the wave's lip is bulk to the mask, so k_psi is the trade between the lattice's noise and the lip's sharpness, the paper's own one free parameter; and `mask_dilate` (one voxel) is our departure, needed because we evolve a signed distance where the paper evolves its density. Before this the pipeline was one Gaussian pass over everything, which erased what is thinner than about two cubes (the crop test of 2026-10-16 in `out/runs/pistonbeach4/renders/crop7/`: every setting only chose between drawing a 25 cm bead as a bead and as fog).
- **The reference and the node disagree on the beach.** The same solitary wave onto the same 1:15 beach (`reference2d/solitary_wave.py` against `experiments/solitary_beach.py`, 2026-10-16): the reference's face turns steep at 4.2 m of water and its lip overhangs at 3.2 m, the node's at 2.8 and 1.7 m, close to Grilli's vertical face at about 2.6 m; the node's crest shoals to 8.3 m where the reference's reaches 7.4. Not the settings (the reference under the production settings moves by half a metre), not the cells (half-metre cells move it by a tenth), not 2D against 3D (the node one cell wide agrees with its 8 m slice), and the reference's solve converges. Not the FLIP blend either, both run 0.97 / 0.9; and the blend is not a knob to turn here: at 0.5 the reference's wave arrives as a bore with a 5.2 m crest, at 0 (pure PIC) it never plunges and reaches the shore 3.6 m high, the blend's viscosity having eaten it on the way (the arms are recorded under the resting-tank entry below). A caveat on the reference's side of the table, found the same night: over those 26 s its surface layer is already thinning (the entry below), so its marks are read off a degrading surface. The gates compared the two at single instants on short scenes; the next step is a lockstep run of the beach with the state compared every few seconds, to see where they part.
- **The parcel's water in the spray.** Since 2026-10-16 the mist draws each escaped particle as the cloud of droplets its water is (`maya/particle_mist`: its radius from the cache, a cloud of `cloud_radius` cells, the extinction of V_p = dx^3 / 8 of water as drops of that radius, splatted additively on a half-cell grid, as PF-FLIP Section 7 rasterises spray). Taken literally that is opaque: two litres of water as 100 um drops in a 25 cm cloud has 15 m^2 of cross-section, and at the paper's own cells (33 cm, 4.5 litres a particle) the same holds, which they do not say how they handle. `mist_density` is therefore the fraction of a parcel's water drawn as droplets of its radius; 1 is the whole parcel and a documented departure from it is a choice. What would make it a measurement: a parcel that leaves the water is partly a blob that falls back and partly spray, and the split is what an atomisation model (the paper's future work: droplet atomisation from CFD) would give. Since the night of 2026-10-16 every escaped droplet is mist and none is surface (the papers' split); the evening's five arms drew a third of the droplets, the rest sitting a voxel deep in their own sphere where the depth rule missed them and the mesher could not draw them, so their fractions read against three times fewer clouds than the same settings draw now.
- **A fourth push-out pass.** The push-out stops once a pass lands outside, so a settled particle sits at or just outside the surface, and a third pass that lands a rounding error short gets no fourth; cheap to add, not a leak.
- **The reference's refinement criterion wraps around the domain** (`band_target` uses `np.roll`), so a band touching one wall also refines the opposite wall; harmless, wasteful. The node mirrors it for lockstep; retire both together and re-record the stage-3a gate.
- **The refinement criterion reads part-filled cells as interface**, which assumes every deep cell keeps its four seeded particles; once motion shuffles them it halves the tree's savings (44 to 22 percent in the resting tank under naive junctions). Candidates: a smoothed fraction or the grid's own phase field, changed in the reference and the node together with the wrap above.
- **The 2D reference's resting tank is not silent, and it blocks long runs.** Its liquid's rms speed grows from 0.10 to 0.54 cells per second over 200 frames under the plain uniform projection. The Bifrost loop does not show it (the same tank's rms decays from 0.013 to 0.003 over 100 s), so it is a trait of the Python stepping, not of the method. Measured properly on 2026-10-16, when the piston gate's 50-second channel lost its water level: a 600 x 40 tank 20 m deep with the plate standing still goes from rest to a liquid rms speed of 1.0 m/s in 20 s with nothing driving it, the grid velocity itself carrying 0.5 m/s of it; the top two metres of water thin from four particles a cell to almost none by 50 s, a thousand air particles sink below 18 m, the deep water packs to 4.45 particles a cell, and the highest liquid particle sinks 2.4 m. One variable per arm in a 200 x 40 tank over 40 s, liquid rms at the end and the fall of the highest particle: the default blend 0.97 / 0.9 gives 1.08 m/s and 1.66 m; blend 0.5 on both phases 0.15 m/s and 0.08 m; blend 0 (pure PIC) 0.06 m/s and nothing; the default seeded without jitter 0.36 m/s and 0.31 m, quiet for 15 s first; the default at substeps of 0.01 s instead of 0.05 s, 0.54 m/s and 0.47 m. So the FLIP blend keeps and feeds the interface's noise until the surface layer is a mixing layer, and a lower blend is no cure, its viscosity damps the waves the gates are after (a 2.7 m wave train is 0.51 of its linear height at blend 0 and 0.64 at 0.5, 100 to 350 m from the paddle). Until the cause is found, a 2D reference run at the default blend is good for about 20 s of quiet water, and the piston gate reads its waves against the surface's own drifting level (the dispersion profile detrended over its window, the beach's crests and marks from the deep water's level at that instant); a count of liquid particles per column was tried as a drift-proof surface and is noisier than the top, the mixing layer shuffles particles sideways too. Candidates to test: the noise the FLIP delta leaves on particles straddling the 1000:1 interface, the splat kernel's width against the interface band, a particle-separation pass.
- **Volume-drift correction** (the paper's divergence correction term): add if a run visibly loses volume; none has so far.
- **Pressure tolerance.** The authors solve to 1e-4 where we run 1e-6; a gated lever on the solve's 40 percent of a substep.

## Answered

- ~~Why does the stock-graph ST plunge produce 56 peak bubbles against the reference's 259?~~ Answered 2026-10-11 (Aniso PR #48): not a solver bug. The validator forwarded only `rho0_face` while the graph's escape parameters defaulted to zero, so the two sides had run different physics since the test was born; with parameters matched the plunge scores droplets 99 against 94 and bubbles 237 against 259. The story is under "Both gaps closed" in the [history](history.md).
- ~~The 20-second bore has zero actual-state obstacle penetrations but 1,225 display-position penetrations.~~ Answered 2026-10-11 (Aniso PRs #43 and #44): the time resynchronization ran after the final obstacle sweep; the synced display position now gets the same push-out, position only, and the re-rendered demo audits at zero.
- ~~Hero scene.~~ Answered 2026-10-13: a tsunami into a coastline corner of a licensed captured scene (milestone 7).
- ~~Preconditioner for the 3D solve: incomplete Cholesky or a multigrid V-cycle.~~ Answered 2026-10-06 (Aniso PR #25): a geometric multigrid V-cycle inside flexible PCG solves the dam-break system in 9 iterations instead of 533, same solution; incomplete Cholesky was never needed.
- ~~Is the stock splat's kernel smooth enough for a quiet phase field?~~ Answered at milestone 4: the stock linear kernel's field matches the reference, and swapping the paper's Eq. 6 kernel for a tent kernel moves the interface midpoint by under 0.01 voxels.
- ~~Junction treatment for the adaptive solve.~~ Settled 2026-10-13: both kept as options, uniform the default; see [decisions](decisions.md).
- ~~Arbitrary solids.~~ Answered 2026-10-14 by the mesh-collider stages 1 to 3 and the showcases; see the [history](history.md).
