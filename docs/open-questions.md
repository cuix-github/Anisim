# Open questions

Kept current; the answered ones stay at the bottom with their answer and date, because the answers are findings.

## Open

- **A CFL-bound substep.** Every long run steps at a fixed dt. The 512 run's air reached 900 to 1450 cells per second and crossed 76 to 123 cells in one substep, past anything the push-out reaches, which is how a handful of air particles ended up inside a boulder for one frame; the 2D long runs' splashes made particles at 120 to 460 cells per second in every arm. Fine for an A/B between solvers, not for a quality claim.
- **Moving colliders, the node.** The 2D reference has its moving solid since 2026-10-16 (`reference2d/validate_piston.py`: a plate sliding in the tank, its speed on the faces it closes, in the divergence of the cut faces it covers and in the push-out, the static solid bit for bit at zero stroke, the wave train against the dispersion relation). The node still has one static signed-distance field per run with velocity zero; the plate's level set rebuilt every substep and its velocity carried the same three ways is the next step, gated in lockstep against the reference. Two lessons from the reference for the node: a wall sweeping into the sealed tank has no pressure solution (the plate stays below the ceiling so the air flows over it), and the plate's speed goes to the plate's faces only, never to the static solid's.
- **The reference and the node disagree on the beach.** The same solitary wave onto the same 1:15 beach (`reference2d/solitary_wave.py` against `experiments/solitary_beach.py`, 2026-10-16): the reference's face turns steep at 4.2 m of water and its lip overhangs at 3.2 m, the node's at 2.8 and 1.7 m, close to Grilli's vertical face at about 2.6 m; the node's crest shoals to 8.3 m where the reference's reaches 7.4. Not the settings (the reference under the production settings moves by half a metre), not the cells (half-metre cells move it by a tenth), not 2D against 3D (the node one cell wide agrees with its 8 m slice), and the reference's solve converges. Not the FLIP blend either, both run 0.97 / 0.9; and the blend is not a knob to turn here: at 0.5 the reference's wave arrives as a bore with a 5.2 m crest, at 0 (pure PIC) it never plunges and reaches the shore 3.6 m high, the blend's viscosity having eaten it on the way (the arms are recorded under the resting-tank entry below). A caveat on the reference's side of the table, found the same night: over those 26 s its surface layer is already thinning (the entry below), so its marks are read off a degrading surface. The gates compared the two at single instants on short scenes; the next step is a lockstep run of the beach with the state compared every few seconds, to see where they part.
- **Rendering droplets and bubbles.** How escaped particles are drawn, decided now that the shot exists: in progress on the `surface-preview` branch as a meshed surface plus a fog volume for the spray.
- **A fourth push-out pass.** The push-out stops once a pass lands outside, so a settled particle sits at or just outside the surface, and a third pass that lands a rounding error short gets no fourth; cheap to add, not a leak.
- **The reference's refinement criterion wraps around the domain** (`band_target` uses `np.roll`), so a band touching one wall also refines the opposite wall; harmless, wasteful. The node mirrors it for lockstep; retire both together and re-record the stage-3a gate.
- **The refinement criterion reads part-filled cells as interface**, which assumes every deep cell keeps its four seeded particles; once motion shuffles them it halves the tree's savings (44 to 22 percent in the resting tank under naive junctions). Candidates: a smoothed fraction or the grid's own phase field, changed in the reference and the node together with the wrap above.
- **The 2D reference's resting tank is not silent, and it blocks long runs.** Its liquid's rms speed grows from 0.10 to 0.54 cells per second over 200 frames under the plain uniform projection. The Bifrost loop does not show it (the same tank's rms decays from 0.013 to 0.003 over 100 s), so it is a trait of the Python stepping, not of the method. Measured properly on 2026-10-16, when the piston gate's 50-second channel lost its water level: a 600 x 40 tank 20 m deep with the plate standing still goes from rest to a liquid rms speed of 1.0 m/s in 20 s with nothing driving it, the grid velocity itself carrying 0.5 m/s of it; the top two metres of water thin from four particles a cell to almost none by 50 s, a thousand air particles sink below 18 m, the deep water packs to 4.45 particles a cell, and the highest liquid particle sinks 2.4 m. One variable per arm in a 200 x 40 tank over 40 s, liquid rms at the end and the fall of the highest particle: the default blend 0.97 / 0.9 gives 1.08 m/s and 1.66 m; blend 0.5 on both phases 0.15 m/s and 0.08 m; blend 0 (pure PIC) 0.06 m/s and nothing; the default seeded without jitter 0.36 m/s and 0.31 m, quiet for 15 s first; the default at substeps of 0.01 s instead of 0.05 s, 0.54 m/s and 0.47 m. So the FLIP blend keeps and feeds the interface's noise until the surface layer is a mixing layer, and a lower blend is no cure, its viscosity damps the waves the gates are after (a 2.7 m wave train is 0.51 of its linear height at blend 0 and 0.64 at 0.5, 100 to 350 m from the paddle). Until the cause is found, a 2D reference run at the default blend is good for about 20 s of quiet water, and the piston gate reads its waves against the surface's own drifting level (the dispersion profile detrended over its window, the beach's crests and marks from the deep water's level at that instant); a count of liquid particles per column was tried as a drift-proof surface and is noisier than the top, the mixing layer shuffles particles sideways too. Candidates to test: the noise the FLIP delta leaves on particles straddling the 1000:1 interface, the splat kernel's width against the interface band, a particle-separation pass.
- **Volume-drift correction** (the paper's divergence correction term): add if a run visibly loses volume; none has so far.
- **Pressure tolerance.** The authors solve to 1e-4 where we run 1e-6; a gated lever on the solve's 40 percent of a substep.

## Answered

- ~~Why does the stock-graph ST plunge produce 56 peak bubbles against the reference's 259?~~ Answered 2026-10-11 (Aniso PR #48): not a solver bug. The validator forwarded only `rho0_face` while the graph's escape parameters defaulted to zero, so the two sides had run different physics since the test was born; with parameters matched the plunge scores droplets 99 against 94 and bubbles 237 against 259. The story is under "Both gaps closed" in the [history](history.md).
- ~~The 20-second bore has zero actual-state obstacle penetrations but 1,225 display-position penetrations.~~ Answered 2026-10-11 (Aniso PRs #43 and #44): the time resynchronization ran after the final obstacle sweep; the synced display position now gets the same push-out, position only, and the re-rendered demo audits at zero.
- ~~Hero scene.~~ Answered 2026-10-13: a tsunami into a coastline corner of a licensed captured scene (milestone 7).
- ~~Preconditioner for the 3D solve: incomplete Cholesky or a multigrid V-cycle.~~ Answered 2026-10-06 (Aniso PR #25): a geometric multigrid V-cycle inside flexible PCG solves the dam-break system in 9 iterations instead of 533, same solution; incomplete Cholesky was never needed.
- ~~Is the stock splat's kernel smooth enough for a quiet phase field?~~ Answered at milestone 4: the stock linear kernel's field matches the reference, and swapping the paper's Eq. 6 kernel for a tent kernel moves the interface midpoint by under 0.01 voxels.
- ~~Junction treatment for the adaptive solve.~~ Settled 2026-10-13: both kept as options, uniform the default; see [decisions](decisions.md).
- ~~Arbitrary solids.~~ Answered 2026-10-14 by the mesh-collider stages 1 to 3 and the showcases; see the [history](history.md).
