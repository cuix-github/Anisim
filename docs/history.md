# History: the milestones and their records

Every finding, number and lesson from the milestone log, in order, with the stage markers as headings. Nothing was shortened; the front page summarizes, this file keeps the record.

## Milestone 1. The spike landed

In short: the two-phase projection builds as a Bifrost SDK node and passes its hydrostatic test. The spike results of 2026-09-30: a 1000:1 density-contrast column returns to rest at the 1e-6 level with the analytic pressure jump; unpreconditioned conjugate gradient needs 211 iterations at 64 cells and 1843 at 256, the measured argument for a preconditioner in 3D.

**Spike landed.** This folder builds and the hydrostatic test passes on a clean checkout. Done when `run_hydro_test.bat` reports max velocity after projection below 1e-4.

## Milestone 2. The 2D reference validates the core loop

In short: `reference2d/pfflip2d.py`, about 300 lines of numpy, runs the core loop on three scenes, hydrostatic rest, a dam break and a Rayleigh-Taylor instability that only a two-phase solver can produce. It is the executable spec every Bifrost stage is checked against.

**2D reference validates the core loop.** Hydrostatic rest, a two-phase dam break, and a Rayleigh-Taylor instability, the last being a two-phase-only phenomenon no single-phase solver can produce. Done when RT fingers develop from a seeded perturbation and the dam break front advances plausibly, with images in the repository.

## Milestone 3. The validation gate

In short: done 2026-09-30. The papers' four core claims were tested in the smallest setups that could falsify them: two-phase cushions trapped air where single-phase FLIP falls through it; particle-carried phase does not diffuse where a grid scalar erodes; the blend-viscosity law holds in form with an implementation-specific constant (about 1/30, not 1/6); mixing is self-limiting at 0.14 percent. Verdicts in `reference2d/VALIDATION.md`.

**Validation gate: the paper's claims tested comparatively.** Done 2026-09-30, passed: see `reference2d/VALIDATION.md` (single-phase FLIP baseline vs two-phase on a sealed trapped-air pocket; interface transport vs a grid scalar; the viscosity law calibrated; mixing measured). One carried instruction: recalibrate the blend-to-viscosity constant per implementation.

## Milestone 4. The phase field from stock nodes

In short: done 2026-09-30. Bifrost's stock splat reproduces the reference's phase field on identical particles, mean difference 0.002, maximum 0.08 in the interface band. The authoring facts that cost debugging time are listed here because they will cost it again otherwise.

**Bifrost graph produces the phase field with stock nodes.** Done 2026-09-30: `phase_field_probe.json` reads the same jittered two-block slab the 2D reference seeds (identical particles via .npy), splats phase with the stock node, and the fields agree, mean |difference| 0.002, maximum 0.08 confined to the one-voxel interface band, interface midpoint within 0.03 voxels. Authoring facts that cost debugging time (see also: every file write inside an iterate must feed the success accumulator or lazy evaluation prunes it, learned twice; the shipped libraries contain equality but no greater/less comparisons; empty float3 arrays do not round-trip through the NumPy file nodes): a new point property needs `set_geo_property` (the `_data` variant only overwrites existing ones); `splat_points_into_volume` writes only into existing tiles, so build the volume from the points first with `points_to_volume`, and it needs fog density on or the volume has no voxels at all; give the splat one property name per node, a two-name filter string misbehaved.

## Milestone 6.5. The adaptive-solve spike pair

In short: built and gated end to end between 2026-10-09 and 2026-10-13, then kept as an option. Spike 1's profile capped a perfect adaptive solve at about one percent of a stock substep; the fused transfer later raised the solve's share to 22 and then 40 percent. Stages 1 to 3c put the adaptive solve in the 2D reference and the 3D node with two junction treatments, naive (the paper's, cheaper, stirs air at rest) and cleanup (exact at rest, a second solve, further from uniform in moving liquid). At finale size the implementation costs 3.7x and 7.1x the uniform pressure phase, mostly setup rebuilt every step; the solve pays only where most of the box is calm. Uniform stays the default.

**The adaptive-solve spike pair**, deliberately after 6 because its multigrid machinery is most of the work already done. Two separate claims, two gates: (a) the solver math in 2D, junction stencils, conservative transfer, convergence on an irregular hierarchy, validated as a numpy adaptive reference against the uniform reference on the gate scenes, then the node in lockstep; (b) the Bifrost foundation, a small probe that stores the adaptive grid in a native multi-depth volume and walks it through the preview-tier traversal API behind a one-file adapter. Honest expectation: little speedup at snug-box scale; the value is proof that the declared limitation is removable, and our own measured evidence for what the source reading said. 3D extension only if a shot demands it.

### Spike 1, the profiling that gates the pair, done 2026-10-09

(`PFFLIP_PROFILE=1` prints per-phase node times; two finale-scale runs of 4 and 12 substeps resumed from the mid-bore overnight state, walls differenced so startup cancels): at 256x64x128 with 6.8M particles a production substep costs 96.4 s of wall, of which the entire custom node is 5.6 s and the multigrid-PCG pressure solve 0.9 s at 23 iterations — 0.9 percent of the substep, 16 percent of the node. Amdahl therefore caps a *perfect* adaptive pressure solve at about one percent of wall-clock. The bill lives in the graph layer (around 91 s per substep: the stock splat families over the particle arrays, plus the per-substep NumPy dumps), and even inside the node the particle pass at 4.5 s outweighs the solve five to one.

Verdict: gate (a) remains available as solver craft, but neither gate buys milestone 7 anything measurable; if the hero scene needs speed, the measured target is the splat layer and the dump policy, not the solve.

### Stage 1 of gate (a) PASSED 2026-10-10

(`reference2d/adaptive_solve.py`, gated by `reference2d/validate_adaptive_solve.py`): finite volume on a graded 2:1 quadtree, junction stencils built variationally (A = G^T beta G, symmetric PSD by construction) with offset-corrected lateral interpolation exact for linear fields in every graded configuration, and the adjoint-consistent right-hand side b = G^T(A u*).

Five checks: refined-everywhere reduces to the 5-point operator at 1.8e-15; the uniform solve matches `pfflip2d.project` itself at 1.7e-14 on a real dam state; measured convergence order 1.99/2.02 ACROSS junctions (the naive 1.5-distance stencil scored 0.90 before the variational rebuild); the 1000:1 hydrostatic column rests at 1e-4 of the gravity kick on an adaptive grid; and on a real dam-break state the drift-vs-band-width curve gives the criterion its validated operating point - fine band >= 5 cells around the interface, 50 percent of unknowns saved, water-face drift at 6e-4 of max speed (the beta ratio protects liquid exactly as predicted), with the known 2-cell air edge-skin reported and shrinking monotonically with band width.

Three lessons for stage 3, each measured the hard way: junction samples must be laterally aligned (the naive stencil's first-order error is exactly dt g/3 in hydrostatics); the divergence must be the operator's own adjoint (mixing conventions cost 6 percent hydro residuals from an operator whose consistency error was 4e-4); and the refinement criterion must respect the 1000:1 density tail and the band-noise skin, not just distance to the interface.

### Stage 2 PASSED 2026-10-11

(`Multigrid` in the same module): a geometric V-cycle over the collapsed-quadtree hierarchy - every level is a stage-1 Operator assembled by the same variational rules, residuals sum into parents, corrections inject into children, production smoother constants - inside flexible PCG. On the stage-1 systems at the same 1e-9 tolerance: dam band-5 323 -> 20 iterations, 1000:1 hydro 180 -> 21, solutions identical to 2e-10, five levels. The adaptive hierarchy converges like the uniform multigrid precedent (533 -> 9); what remains for stage 3 is the 3D node and its receipts.

### Stage 3a PASSED 2026-10-11

(`reference2d/adaptive_projection.py`, gated by `reference2d/validate_adaptive_projection.py`): the adaptive solve inside the running 2D simulation, in place of `Sim.project`. A running simulation asks more of the solve than a frozen state does, because the particles read velocities from the uniform face grid everywhere: every uniform face must leave the projection corrected, including the faces inside a coarse cell where the adaptive solve has a single pressure, and every fine cell's inflow must equal its outflow. Two findings shaped the module. The stage-1 variational junction stencil balances flux only in a weighted sense, so its uniform faces keep a divergence of 1.8 per step next to junctions on the gate's dam state, where the uniform projection leaves 8e-7.

And the faces inside coarse cells need a real local solve: leaving them alone or interpolating gradients across the cell leaves 0.2 to 0.8. Four junction treatments were measured and two survive, both kept, because choosing between them is a 3D cost-versus-quality question. *naive* is the paper's own choice (Losasso et al. 2004): one solve, exact books, first order at junctions. *cleanup* (the default) runs the variational solve, then a naive-stencil projection of only its leftover: exact books, and the first-order error now acts on that small leftover instead of on gravity, for the price of a second solve.

Rejected, with the code in the PR's first commit: the variational solve alone (its books do not balance), and moving the leftover junction flux sideways without a projection (exact books, but it adds energy: the dam break's air reached 347 cells per second by frame 23, against about 25 in every other arm). Every gate bar is set by the uniform solver's own particle-seed spread, how far two uniform runs differing only in their random seeding land apart; an arm passes inside twice that. Books: 8.7e-7 (naive) and 1.6e-6 (cleanup) against uniform's 8.3e-7. The 160x80 dam break: both arms inside the bar on front, centroid, occupied area and per-phase kinetic energy, with 61 percent of unknowns saved; cleanup's tightest margin is the centroid, 0.013 cells against a 0.016 bar.

At rest (a half-full 64x64 tank, three seeds) the two arms part: cleanup matches uniform seed by seed (air rms 1.00-1.01x, liquid 1.03-1.04x), while naive's first-order junction error stirs the air at 5.2-5.6x uniform's rms by frame 40, still growing at frame 200 (0.58 against 0.09 cells per second) - a parasitic current the paper does not report. Cost per projection: naive 13.0 iterations in the tank and 19.5 in the dam break, cleanup 19.7 and 33.0 (13.6 + 6.1 and 18.3 + 14.8), so +52 and +69 percent. The choice between them moves to the stage-3c receipts on the canonical finale state: solve cost at 1e-6 and 1e-4, accuracy against uniform, the tank at rest, and side-by-side frames with the junctions drawn in.

### Stage 3b PASSED 2026-10-12

(`nodes/src/PFFlipAdaptive.cpp` behind `step_3d`'s new `pressure_solver` port, gated by `gates/validate_adaptive_step.py`): the adaptive solve in the C++ node, naive junctions only. The build order was settled after stage 3a: naive first, because cleanup contains it and cleanup's extra part, the second-order junction stencil, is the hardest code in a 3D octree; and since cleanup behaved like the uniform solve in 2D, stage 3c compares naive against the uniform 3D solve that already exists, and cleanup gets built only if naive's stirring shows.

The port is written for 3D, an octree whose cells stay one fine cell thick at nz=1, and at nz=1 it reproduces the 2D reference step for step on three states - the dam break at frames 12 and 30 and a resting tank - with identical pressure iteration counts (20, 19, 13), identical divergence after projection, and faces within 1e-6 (float32 port rounding). Two behaviors of the reference had to be mirrored to get there, both now named in the code. Its refinement criterion compares and grows cells periodically across the domain (`np.roll`). And its multigrid coarsening is not a partition: a parent whose children were a mix of leaves and subdivided cells collapses next to the collapsed subdivided child, inside its own footprint, so neighbors must be found by exact cell keys rather than by which cell covers a point (the point-cover lookup reached the same answer in 32 iterations instead of 20).

With `pressure_solver` 0, the default, the node is bit-identical to main's: the threading experiment's gate mode, run with a pack built from main and then with this one, saved the same 774 arrays across its 20 graph runs (flat and 3D loops with adaptive particles, ST, obstacles and dump cadence), and `validate_3d.py` writes byte-identical outputs and an identical log. Obstacles stay with the uniform solve for now.

### Stage 3b2 PASSED 2026-10-12

(the same files, `pressure_solver` 2): the cleanup junction in the node at nz=1, so the two treatments can be compared head to head inside Bifrost instead of through the uniform solve as cleanup's stand-in, and an artist keeps the choice. It adds stage 1's variational junction stencil to the operator on every multigrid level - the coarse side is read where the fine cell's center sits, 3/4 the coarse cell plus 1/4 its lateral neighbor, with that neighbor at the same level, one level coarser, or refined into an aligned pair - and then runs the naive-stencil cleanup solve; everything else is stage 3b's machinery.

The lockstep gate now runs both junctions on four cases: the three before, plus the frame-30 dam with band 1 and three levels, whose tree corners put a junction's lateral neighbor one level coarser (16 junctions on the leaf level, 7 on the first multigrid level), a case the default trees never build. Every case matches the reference's iteration count - for cleanup, both solves together, 34, 32, 45 and 24, against naive's 20, 19, 25 and 13 - and its divergence after projection, with faces within 1e-6. The variational sample reads one lateral axis, so 2 at nz>1 falls back to the uniform solve with a one-time note until stage 3c builds the octree's two-axis sample.

The obstacle fallback now counts only boxes that reach the domain: the loop drivers always pass a placeholder box far outside it, which marks no face and holds no particle but had counted as an obstacle, so every adaptive run through a loop graph would have quietly used the uniform solve. On a single step the placeholder is now byte-identical to no box for 0, 1 and 2, while a box inside the domain still gives exactly the uniform result with the note. With `pressure_solver` 0 and 1 the node is byte-identical to main's on all four cases, every output array and reported stat; and the default path stays bit-identical to main: the threading experiment's gate mode saved the same arrays as main's baseline across its 20 graph runs, and `validate_3d.py` writes the same log and byte-identical arrays in both trees.

### The long three-way run in Bifrost, 2026-10-12

(`experiments/adaptive_long_run.py`; results and charts in `results/adaptive/long_run_*`): uniform, naive and cleanup side by side at nz=1 through the fused loop graph, three particle seeds each, on the stage-3a scenes, with the uniform runs' seed spread as the noise bar. The loop graphs gained the solver ports and a per-frame velocity dump; preparing the run surfaced the placeholder-obstacle fallback fixed above. *The resting tank, 2000 frames (100 s):* cleanup is indistinguishable from uniform on every seed (air and liquid rms 0.86-1.0x, inside the bar throughout), while naive's air stirring grows without levelling off - 0.35 cells per second at frame 100, 0.85 at 500, 1.49 at 2000, 430x uniform's, the growth slowing roughly like the square root of time - into two counter-rotating cells that sweep the air particles out of the top corners.

Its liquid moves at 3x uniform's rms (0.009 cells per second, invisible at this scale), its fastest particle at 1.15 against uniform's 0.34-0.47, and the stirring shuffles the liquid particles until only 40 percent of the deep cells hold their four: the refinement criterion then reads part-filled cells as interface, and the tree's savings fall from 44 to 22 percent. The 2D record's naive air at frame 200 (0.58) reappears here (0.56); the reference's resting-liquid growth does not (uniform's liquid rms decays from 0.013 to 0.003). *The dam break, 1000 frames (60 s):* every arm stays inside the uniform seed bar at every checkpoint and the liquid curves nearly coincide; cleanup's air runs calmer after the splash (0.5-0.6x uniform's, just inside the bar); pressure iterations per substep are 36 uniform, 40 naive and 60 cleanup.

Both scenes run a fixed step (0.025 s in the tank, the reference's step at rest; 0.015 s in the dam break, CFL 0.5 at the expected 30 cells per second), and the dam break's extreme particles - liquid up to 120-180 and air up to 340-460 cells per second in every arm, uniform included - say its violent phase is under-resolved in time without a CFL limit, alike for the three arms.

### Stage 3c, first half, PASSED 2026-10-12

(`gates/validate_adaptive_3d.py`): both junctions in the 3D octree. Cleanup's junction sample generalizes by correcting along each tangent axis separately - the coarse value plus, per tangent, the 2D weight times the lateral difference (1/2 + 1/4 + 1/4 at one level) - which is exact for linear pressure without the edge neighbor the 2:1 grading does not reach, and at nz=1 is the 2D sample exactly (outputs byte-identical to stage 3b2's on the four lockstep cases).

With no 3D reference, the gate checks reductions, structure and anchors: a tree refined everywhere reproduces the uniform solve (2e-10 at a 1e-10 tolerance); every cleanup junction face of a 3D dam tree reproduces linear pressure along x, y and z (to 4e-15, read through the new `PFFLIP_ADAPTIVE_DUMP`); divergence after projection sits at solver level for both junctions; a z-invariant flow's middle slab reproduces the nz=1 solve (5e-5 naive, 2e-4 cleanup); and a half-full 32x64x32 tank at rest stays still under cleanup (5e-7 of the gravity kick) while naive's junctions leave 0.42 of the kick (0.30 at nz=1, the 2D record's dt g/3; the 3D excess sits along coarse-region edges, where two lateral offsets add). One finding changes the naive-versus-cleanup picture: in moving liquid inside coarse cells, cleanup lands further from the uniform solve than naive.

A divergence-free swirl's liquid faces differ from uniform by 0.32 percent rms under cleanup against 0.18 under naive at nz=1, and 0.51 against 0.19 in 3D, the 3D excess concentrated beside the z walls. The variational solve's books are why: by naive's measure its corrected field keeps about as much divergence as it started with (1.03x at nz=1, 1.16x in 3D), so the cleanup projection is a full second solve, not a touch-up, and with far more junctions in 3D (3456 against 100 on these trees) the two interact more. Cleanup is exact at rest; it is not closer to uniform in motion. The second half of stage 3c, the cost receipts, has a blocker: the canonical profiling state is the bore with two pillars, and obstacles still send the adaptive solve back to the uniform one.

An indicative receipt stands in (`experiments/adaptive_cost.py`, `results/adaptive/cost_indicative.json`): one step at the finale's 256x64x128 on a synthetic dam with a bed and a swirl, no obstacles, timed inside the node (`PFFLIP_PROFILE`, which now also prints the adaptive solve's parts). The tree keeps 0.94M of the 2.1M cells, yet the pressure phase costs 612 ms uniform, 2237 naive and 4336 cleanup at 1e-6 (502, 2208 and 3939 at 1e-4; medians of three). Most of naive's time is setup rebuilt every step - the tree 0.23 s and the multigrid hierarchy 1.2 s, built through hash maps and per-row lists - and cleanup builds a second hierarchy for its second solve.

That part is implementation, not method; but naive's solve itself (29 iterations, 0.54 s) is only about a tenth under uniform's whole pressure phase, so at this scale an adaptive solve breaks even at best, as spike 1's Amdahl bound predicted.

## Milestone 6.75. The fused transfer layer

In short: 2026-10-09, the one reversal of stock-first. Profiling put the stock splat layer at 94 percent of a substep, so one custom node now scatters every channel in a single pass: 96.4 s a substep to 10.22 s at finale scale, and the dam-break front gap against the reference halved to 1.0 cell because the kernel is now the reference's. The exact pie of a fused substep, measured 2026-10-10: 10.3 s = particle pass 4.6 + scatter 3.0 + pressure solve 2.3 + the rest.

**The fused transfer layer**, 2026-10-09, one day after the spike-1 profile (Aniso PR #37) put a price on the stock-splat convenience and flipped the stock-first posture for the one data path that dominated the bill. P2G moved from thirty stock `splat_points_into_volume` plus thirty `sample_volume` invocations per substep into one custom node, `PFFlip::Solve::p2g_3d`, which scatters every channel - mass, per-axis momentum, phase, the ST temporal weight - onto all three face grids and both size tiers in a single pass, straight into the flat arrays the step node consumes.

The kernel is now the reference's Eq. 6 on the reference's face frames, so the pipeline moved *closer* to the paper, not further: the dam-break gate's historical 1.9-cell front gap against the reference - stock-kernel mismatch all along - halved to 1.0 cell and the centroid drift fell from 1.24 to 0.35 cells, with hydro-column grid divergence at 2.6e-7 (unit gate `validate_p2g.py`: reference operator at float rounding, independent 3D numpy at 2.4e-4, axis permutation bit-exact; system gate `validate_p2g_loop.py`).

Receipts, resumed from the same mid-bore finale state as the spike with the stock baseline re-reproduced within 3 percent the same day: 96.4 s per substep to 10.22 s, **9.4x** at 256x64x128 with 6.8M particles (`bench_p2g_finale.py`), and 8.8x on the 20-second production bore-with-pillars demo whose side-by-side video with live compute clocks lives in `results/p2g/` (`demo_p2g_speed.py`). `step_3d` untouched, channel semantics drop-in, every prior gate standing; the eps-inversion trick now reconstructs sums the fused node computed exactly, earmarked for retirement at the next cleanup. The scatter is serial and deterministic; threading it is a measured decision for later, not a reflex.

### The exact pie, measured 2026-10-10

(`dump_gate` + P2GPROF landed with it; the full receipts are in the Aniso PR #40 description): a fused finale substep is 10.3 s = particle pass 4.6 + scatter 3.0 + pressure solve 2.3 + assemble/correct 0.2 + graph residual 0.3, and the per-substep dumps cost no measurable time — the `dump_every` port's value is disk, not clock. The solve is 2.3 s here, not spike 1's 0.9 s, for an understood reason: the reference's r = 1 kernel draws a sharper interface than the radius-1.5 stock splat, and the harsher beta contrast takes 33-55 MG-PCG iterations where stock needed 13-15 — fidelity bought at solver expense. Net position: the solve already carries 22 percent of the substep, the neighborhood the ST-FLIP authors' own Table 1 reports (26 percent, on 32 threaded cores with their adaptive multigrid on), so the pipeline already has the paper's cost shape.

Agreed order from here: deterministic threading of the scatter and the particle pass (the two big slices; per-core our stage costs are within about 2x of the authors', so the missing factor is literally their 32 threads), then milestone 6.5a with its production case in hand. A further gated lever: the authors solve pressure to 1e-4 where we run 1e-6.

## Both gaps closed, 2026-10-11

In short: both open gates closed by forensics, not by loosening thresholds. The display positions inside pillars were the missing resync clamp; the bubble-count gate had run different escape physics on its two sides since it was born. Ten of twelve validators had printed FAIL while exiting zero; every one now carries a real exit code.

## Mesh colliders and the showcases (opened 2026-10-13)

In short: any mesh is a collider now. Stage 1 (2026-10-13) put level-set solids with binary faces or cut cells into the 2D reference, stage 2 (2026-10-13) into the node with exact face areas and a 1 percent cut-off that removes sliver spikes, stage 3 (2026-10-14) built the graph from the shipped collider node through public nodes only. The 256 showcase ran overnight 2026-10-14 (4.13 h, no penetrations) and turned out to be on the stock-splat graph, so the fused graph became the default; the 512 run of 2026-10-15 simulated 20.38 s in 2.40 h with 52 million particles and every substep kept.

Arbitrary solids, opened 2026-10-13: both solves take only axis-aligned boxes, and the adaptive solve takes none (any box reaching the domain sends it to the uniform solve). The hero coastline needs mesh colliders. Shipped public nodes cover the conversion: `mesh_to_level_set` voxelizes a mesh into a signed distance field (solid mode, `min_hole_radius` bridging small holes and self-intersections, `thickening` keeping thin features), `sample_volume` and `sample_volume_gradient` read the distance and the outward direction, and the OpenVDB read and write nodes cache a static collider; Bifrost's own liquid documentation carries colliders the same way, as a signed collider distance. Ours to build: faces fully, partly or not open by the solid they cut, consistent with the two-phase weights, and the particle push-out.

Order: the 2D reference first, where a box-shaped distance field must reproduce today's box path exactly, then curved shapes against known answers, then the node and the loop graph. In the adaptive solve, cells next to solids stay fine, as the paper keeps resolution boundaries off the liquid surface, so the collider code there is the uniform code.

### Stage 1 PASSED 2026-10-13 in the 2D reference

(`reference2d/validate_level_set_solids.py`): `pfflip2d.Sim` takes `solid_phi`, the distance at the grid nodes, with binary faces (the boxes' midpoint rule) or `solid_cut` (open fractions), and pushes particles out by a Newton step along the field's gradient, losing their normal velocity; without a field every path is bit-identical to before. Grid-aligned boxes close exactly the faces the box path closes, binary and cut; off the grid the two differ only at box corners (3 faces of 1,038), where the field bends between two nodes, and a half-cell slab vanishes between the nodes altogether (35 faces to none: `mesh_to_level_set`'s thickening is the remedy at that level).

On the bore against the block (which arrives at about 4 s, not at the 1.5 s `gates/validate_solids.py` assumes: that gate's lockstep step has never pushed a particle out of the block, and should move to 4.5 s), one step at the block is bit-identical on the grid and apart for one particle, in a corner cell, where the field pushes diagonally rather than along the box's least-penetration axis; over 6 s and three seeds the centroid, the splash height at the block and the overtopped share all sit inside the box path's seed bar, with nothing left inside after any push-out. Two lessons the node must carry.

First, the support share: a face partly covered by the solid collects only part of a full face's splat mass, since the solid holds no particles, so its phase read as half air (0.46 on faces under a quarter open, against 0.98) and air's pressure coefficient made those faces the strongest in the solve - cut cells stirred still water in a round bowl at 3x binary's (rms 0.53 against 0.18 cells per second); reading each face's mass against the share of its splat reach outside the solid, measured once by a fine all-liquid probe as `calibrate()` measures a full face, brings it to 0.19, beside the square tank's 0.17. Second, the Newton push-out: in a corner cell the bilinear field is a product whose slope can be a tenth of a distance's, so a unit step left up to three particles inside; the Newton step clears them and is the old arithmetic wherever the field is a true distance.

Cut cells earn their place on slopes: a block released on a frictionless 30-degree incline accelerates at 0.99 of g sin(theta) with them and 0.94 on the staircase. Next, the node: a face's open area from its four corners in 3D, the support share computed once per static collider, lockstep at nz=1 against this reference, then the graph from `mesh_to_level_set`.

### Stage 2 PASSED 2026-10-13 in the node

(`gates/validate_level_set_node.py`): `step_3d` takes `solid_phi` (the distance at the grid nodes) and `solid_mode` (1 binary faces, 2 cut cells); the 3D loop graphs read it once per run (`path_solid_phi`, `solid_mode`), and `graphs/loop3d.py` passes `solid=(phi, mode)`. A face's open area in 3D comes from its four corners as four triangles around the centre: exact for any plane (within 2e-16 of the clipped area, on every face kind of three tilted planes), bit-identical to the reference's faces at nz=1, and the same in every slab of a z-invariant field.

A grid-aligned box written as a field gives the box path's grid velocities and pressure bit for bit, binary and cut, at nz=1 on the bore at the block and in 3D on a pillar, with particles differing only beside the box's corners and edges; against the reference's twin at nz=1 the node agrees to float rounding, binary on the bore and cut cells on a slope. The support share turned out to be the reference's need, not the node's: the node's phase is the mean of the particle phases a face's kernel reaches (the stock-splat meaning the fused P2G kept), so a face the solid half covers still reads liquid. In still water in a round bowl none of 107 submerged, partly open faces reads below 0.9 that way, against 90 with the reference's raw-mass phase and still 29 with the share.

One new rule, found in 3D and adopted by the reference too: cut cells close a face less than 1% open (`Sim.SOLID_MIN_OPEN`, `Solids::kMinOpen`). Where the water surface meets a curved wall the solve barely sees such slivers, and from water at rest it left up to 3 cells/s on them, which the particles beside them picked up: a half-full spherical bowl's peak speed reached 5.75 cells/s with cut cells against 0.68 with binary faces. Swept against 5% and 10%, 1% is the smallest cutoff that removes the spikes (peak 0.99) and costs the slopes least (0.986 to 0.980 of g sin(theta) in 3D, nothing flat); the 2D record above is unchanged by it, number for number.

In the node's own loop: at nz=1, still water in a round bowl stays as still as in a square tank (rms 0.014 binary and cut, 0.013 the tank), the block on the 30-degree slope reaches 0.99 of g sin(theta) with cut cells and 0.94 on the staircase (the reference's numbers), and the bore against the block as a field sits inside the box path's seed bar on all four measures; in 3D, still water over a submerged ball stays as still as without it (0.0126 against 0.0124), and a block on a slope tilted off every axis reaches 0.98 of g sin(theta) with cut cells against 0.88 on the staircase. No particle is inside a solid in any dumped frame.

Cost, on one 96x48x64 step with 2.3 million particles (8 threads, median of 5, `experiments/level_set_cost.py`): the face fractions add 2 to 4 ms to the assembly and the whole step stays within run-to-run noise of no solid (199 to 209 ms against 210); a first version cost 60 ms more, until the push-out learned to settle outside particles without the gradient and to leave unjittered display positions alone. Without a field every existing gate's arrays are byte-identical to main's (six gates, 1,892 arrays, both loop graphs regenerated with the new ports). `gates/validate_solids.py` now takes its lockstep step at t = 4.5 s, where the bore meets the block.

### Stage 3 PASSED 2026-10-14, the graph

(`graphs/collider_field.json`, generated by `graphs/generate_collider_graph.py`, gated by `gates/validate_collider_graph.py`): from a mesh to `solid_phi` with stock nodes only, and the artist's input is the shipped `Simulation::Common::collider` node, chosen over a compound of our own for compatibility with existing liquid setups (it costs nothing: the node only tags the mesh with its settings, and the field is computed once).

Reading the shipped liquid solver's own graph showed what that node stores and how the solver voxelizes it, all through public nodes, so the adapter compound `collider_field` does the same: per collider it reads the geometry and settings the node stored (detail size and its resolution mode, offset, thickening, volume mode, hole radius, enable), voxelizes with `compute_voxel_scale` and `mesh_to_volume` as a level set, samples `voxel_signed_distance` at our grid nodes, divides by the cell size, and keeps the minimum over colliders; the node lattice is built in the graph from exact long indices, so any grid size works. `collider_setup.json` wraps it for bifcmd (meshes from .npy, the settings as ports, `solid_phi.npy` out, the loop graphs' `path_solid_phi`), driven by `graphs/colliders.py`, so a static solid is computed once and read by every run.

What the stock level set is, measured: within one cell of the surface it is exact (a sphere to 0.007 cells, a box to 0.0000), which is the band the node reads for face areas and push-out; within two cells it holds to 0.6 (the slack at a box's edges and mid-planes, where two faces are equidistant); the sign is right at every node; beyond that it is a swept far field, exact straight into a wall and 0.8x to 1.2x along diagonals, which the solver never reads. Rebuilding or resizing the level set makes the band worse, not better, so the plain conversion stands. The shipped node's knobs do what they say: thickening moves the surface out by that many voxels, offset by that many scene units, both to 1e-4, and a detail size of two cells or half a cell keeps the surface in place; two colliders give the minimum of the two fields exactly.

On the stage-2 pillar as a mesh, a quarter cell off the nodes and face centres (on them the decision is a toss-up, and the level set's 1e-7 rounding flips a face the analytic field's exact zero closes), binary faces give the analytic field's face areas and one step's grid velocities bit for bit, and cut cells agree within 5e-5 on areas and 5e-4 cells/s on velocities; the flow at 25 cells/s cuts 73 and 113 particles into the pillar and the step pushes every one out. That last check is new ground: stage 2's 3D loops were still water or a block sliding along a plane, so no 3D push-out had been exercised until now (`PFFLIP_SOLID_DEBUG=1` makes the node report its count). One trap found on the way, now guarded in the gate helper: the field's port is float32, and a float64 array reads as empty, so the node ignores the solid with only a note on stderr.

Still water over a mesh ball from the graph stays as still as over the analytic one (0.0126 binary and cut against the tank's 0.0124, nothing inside). Known limit: static colliders only; a moving collider needs the field per frame plus its velocity, which the shipped solver also builds every frame, and is deferred.

### Junction treatment for the 3D adaptive solve, 2026-10-11 to 2026-10-13

Junction treatment for the 3D adaptive solve, opened 2026-10-11 by stage 3a: *naive* (the paper's choice: one solve, but air at rest stirred at 5x uniform and still growing after 200 frames in 2D) or *cleanup* (matches uniform at rest, a second solve, +52 to +69 percent iterations in 2D). Media-and-entertainment work often accepts error for speed, so this is decided by measured evidence in our own scenes, not by taste: the stage-3c receipts measure cost on the canonical finale state at 1e-6 and 1e-4, accuracy against uniform, the tank at rest, and side-by-side frames near the junctions. Build order settled 2026-10-12: naive is built first (stage 3b) and compared against the uniform 3D solve, which stands in for cleanup since the two matched in 2D; cleanup's second-order junction stencil gets built in 3D only if naive's stirring shows.

Revised the same day: a stand-in is an assumption in 3D, so cleanup is built too, as `pressure_solver` 2 (stage 3b2, nz=1), and stage 3c builds the octree for both. The hard part in 3D is cleanup's junction sample: the fine cell sits off the coarse axis in two directions, so the sample blends four cells, one of which touches the coarse cell only along an edge, where the 2:1 grading does not reach. There is no 3D reference to lockstep against, but the sample exists to be exact for linear pressure and a tank at rest has exactly that, so a 3D still tank checks it to rounding.

On naive's stirring, the 2D record so far: the air's growth is linear, +0.11 to +0.13 cells per second every 50 frames with no acceleration, so a slow leak rather than a blow-up, but it had not levelled off by frame 200, and the liquid moves only about 8 percent more than uniform's (whose own resting noise grows, the open question below). A long three-way run in Bifrost - uniform, naive and cleanup at nz=1 - settles whether it plateaus and whether it shows. It ran the same night: over 100 s at rest naive's air does not plateau (1.49 cells per second and still rising, slower) and it shows - as organized air circulation and depleted corners rather than in the water - while cleanup stays with uniform; in the dam break's splash the three are indistinguishable within the seed spread. Iterations per substep: tank 8 uniform, 12 naive, 20.5 cleanup; dam 36, 40, 60.

Stage 3c added the other side of the scale: cleanup is exact at rest in 3D too, but in moving liquid inside coarse cells it lands further from uniform than naive (0.51 against 0.19 percent rms on a 3D swirl), so the choice is quiet water at rest against fidelity in motion, weighed over whole runs rather than single steps. Cost at finale size in the current implementation: naive 3.7x and cleanup 7.1x uniform's pressure phase, mostly per-step setup. Settled 2026-10-13 as the user's choice rather than ours: both stay as gated options and uniform (0) stays the default. Where an adaptive solve pays at all (the grid-adaptivity position below), 1 is the cheaper and 2 keeps air at rest clean; naive's stirring shows only where something rides the air, and the paper's showcase effects (mist and spray carried by the air) are exactly that.

### Grid adaptivity, the position's history, 2026-10-05 to 2026-10-13

Grid adaptivity, position updated 2026-10-05: no longer a flat non-goal but a staged spike (milestone 6.5), after the uniform 3D build stands. Hierarchical depth is first-class in the volume container and cooperative at the conversion edges, but the depth-aware solving itself, halo exchange across levels, conservative transfer, mixed-resolution convergence, is ours to build and as yet untested by us, so it gets the same spike-then-gate discipline as everything else. The production path remains uniform solve plus adaptive particles in a snug box. Position updated again 2026-10-09 by milestone 6.5's spike 1: the profiled pressure share at finale scale is 0.9 percent of wall, so the adaptive solve is officially craft, not production path.

Position updated a third time 2026-10-10, after the fused transfer layer (milestone 6.75) shrank the denominator and its sharper kernel raised the solve's own cost: the solve now carries 22 percent of a fused substep — the same share the authors' own per-stage table reports — so milestone 6.5a is back to being a production proposition, sequenced after deterministic threading. The lesson worth keeping from the 0.9-to-22 journey: a share is set by the whole pie, so never quote one from measurements taken in different pipeline configurations.

Position completed 2026-10-10 by the solve-sweep threading (stacked on Aniso PR #42's transfer threading): with every stage threaded and outputs bit-identical to the serial build, a finale substep is 3.66 s - 26x the week's starting point - and the solve carries 40 percent of it (1.5 s: serial-by-design dot products, bandwidth-bound sweeps, and the sharp-kernel iteration count). That 40 percent against a fully threaded baseline is the production case milestone 6.5a now inherits, measured the same way the authors measured theirs. Position updated a fourth time 2026-10-13, by stage 3c's cost receipt and a re-read of the paper: at snug-box scale the adaptive solve does not pay, and the paper never claimed it would.

Its own introduction calls uniform grids "sufficient for grid resolutions of several hundred cells per dimension" and motivates adaptivity by domains far beyond that; its Table 7 counts active pressure cells as FLIP particles divided by 8, which keeps 2 to 6 percent of the effective cells in its large scenes (1.9 percent of 6144x2048x1600 for the asteroid, 5.2 percent of 3072x1536x1024 for the ocean). At billions of cells adaptivity is feasibility, not speed: a uniform grid that size does not fit in a workstation's memory. And their grid (MSBG, tree-less large blocks) is built to keep an adaptive cell nearly as cheap as a uniform one - 90 percent of streaming bandwidth on a seven-point Laplacian against VDB's 11 (their Table 3) - where ours is a tree rebuilt through hash maps every step. Our synthetic finale-size state keeps 45 percent of its cells.

The break-even kept share, derived from the receipt (uniform 0.29 microseconds per cell; naive 2.37 and cleanup 4.59 per kept cell) and assuming the per-cell cost stays flat as grids grow: naive wins below about 12 percent and cleanup below about 6 today; with the tree and hierarchies free, about 35 and 20 (cleanup's second hierarchy is estimated, its timer lumps the whole pass). So the rule is the calm share of the box, not toy against production: a snug box around the action stays uniform; a wide box that is mostly calm water and open air is where an adaptive solve can win, and the node already knows the share once its tree is built.

Two limits on what that win means: the solve is 40 percent of a substep, so even a free solve speeds a step at most 1.67x; and the paper's large wins come from particles and transfer living on the adaptive grid too (big particles in big cells), which our design keeps on the fine uniform grid. The decisive test is the hero coastline, and it waits on two things in order: mesh colliders (open question below), then the kept share counted on the shot's starting state as the gate for finishing the adaptive work (solids in the tree, the refinement criterion, setup reused between steps).
